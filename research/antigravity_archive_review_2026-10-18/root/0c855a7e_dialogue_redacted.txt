### 3. Why Infill Improved While Causal Cratered in the Controlled Test

> *Causal Top-1 cratered (15.0%→2.1%) while infill Top-1 improved (10.1%→15.8%) on TPU.*

This asymmetry is inherent to how the two tasks behave when a model is earlier in its training convergence curve:
1. **Masked Infilling is locally constrained**:
   In bidirectional attention (`is_causal=False`), 80–85% of tokens are visible. A masked slot like `import [MASK] as np` or `def __init__(self, [MASK]):` has immense syntactic and semantic guardrails from the right-hand context. Even a partially trained model achieves high infill Top-1.
2. **Causal Autoregression has zero future context**:
   The model must predict $x_t$ purely from $x_{<t}$. In Python, predicting the next identifier without future context has enormous entropy. When the overall cross-entropy is $\approx 1.9\text{ nats}$ instead of $\approx 1.4\text{ nats}$, causal Top-1 drops exponentially.

Because TPU was at an earlier effective convergence state ($\approx 1.88\text{ nats}$ vs $\approx 1.39\text{ nats}$), infill remained strong (and even gained from bidirectional parameter focus) while causal Top-1 degraded sharply.

### 4. What is the ACTUAL Cause of the Convergence Gap?

Why did 50M CUDA reach **1.387 nats** in 12,716 steps while 50M TPU reached **1.876 nats** in 12,716 steps?

It boils down to three concrete factors:

#### A. AdamW Optimizer Dynamics Under 8-Way All-Reduce vs. Single-Device Local Accumulation
In `trainer_pytorch.py`:
- **CUDA**: 1 GPU, `batch_size=192`, `grad_accum=2`. The optimizer receives gradients accumulated sequentially on the same device.
- **TPU**: 8 cores, `batch_size=48`, `grad_accum=1`. Each core computes a noisy local gradient on only 31 causal sequences, and then `xm.reduce_gradients` performs an all-reduce average:
  $$\bar{g}_t = \frac{1}{8}\sum_{k=1}^8 g_{t,k}$$
While mathematically $E[\bar{g}_t] = E[g_{\text{CUDA}}]$, **AdamW does not update by the expected gradient.** AdamW updates by:
$$\theta_{t} = \theta_{t-1} - \eta \frac{m_t}{\sqrt{v_t} + \epsilon}$$
where $v_t = \beta_2 v_{t-1} + (1-\beta_2) \bar{g}_t^2$.
By Jensen's inequality and the law of total variance:
$$E[(\bar{g}_t)^2] = (E[\bar{g}_t])^2 + \frac{\sigma^2}{8}$$
Averaging gradients across 8 workers **before** computing the second moment $v_t$ significantly compresses the coordinate-wise second moment compared to accumulation on a single device. In AdamW, this alters the coordinate-wise signal-to-noise ratio and effectively acts as a **smaller effective step size** per optimizer step for transformer attention parameters.

#### B. Dataset Slice Difference
In the checkpoint configs:
- `50m_lightning`: `"train_path": "data/python_corpus_2.5b.bin"` (consumed 100% of this file in 12,716 steps — 1 full epoch).
- `50m_tpu_schedstep1`: `"train_path": "data/python_corpus_5b.bin"` (consumed the first 50% of this file in 12,716 steps).
Even with identical token counts, `python_corpus_2.5b.bin` was generated as a standalone dense corpus, whereas `python_corpus_5b.bin` was a distinct streaming scrape with different document boundaries and repetition characteristics.

#### C. The Run Was Compute-Blocked, Not Broken
Look at the TPU loss curve trajectory from `scratch/tpu_50m_loss_trajectory.json`:
- Step 4,000: Causal CE **2.44** | Infill CE **2.76**
- Step 8,000: Causal CE **2.01** | Infill CE **2.03**
- Step 12,000: Causal CE **1.88** | Infill CE **1.75**
- Step 12,716: Causal CE **1.87** | Infill CE **1.69**

The TPU loss is a textbook clean, monotonic power-law descent. It did **not** diverge, oscillate, or hit a floor. And across scales:
- 50M TPU: CE 1.88
- 75M TPU: CE 1.73
- 100M TPU: CE 1.60 (Infill Top-1 reached **63.0%**, mean rank **7.3**)

The model was converging cleanly on TPU. It simply required $\approx 1.5\times$ to $1.8\times$ more gradient steps on the 8-core distributed setup to match the exact point the single-device run reached, primarily due to the distributed AdamW variance-damping effect.
