### Summary of the Paper
While autoregressive (AR) language models dominate code generation, Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs) offer an alternative through bidirectional context and iterative non-monotonic decoding. In this work, we present télos, a narrow-domain MDLM for Python code autocomplete, and investigate its capability formation and scaling dynamics:
1. Token-Budget Scaling Dynamics: We observe preliminary evidence that the optimal token-to-parameter ratio for MDLMs increases with scale (e.g., 12.5M peaks at 1:15 ratio, while 25M continues improving past 1:25), contrasting with standard constant-ratio Chinchilla scaling in AR models.
2. Contextual Probing vs Aggregate Loss: Aggregate cross-entropy hides sharp, localized capability transitions; structural token syntax closure improves significantly earlier than semantic identifier recovery.
3. Engineering & Hardware Optimizations: We detail empirical corrections, loss reweighting (1/t), and hardware-specific optimizations (MPS/MLX / GPU memory alignment) required for reliable MDLM training.
