### 1. The 100M AR Mystery: Why 1:20 was at ~5 CE and 1:40 was at 1.3 CE

Under standard autoregressive language modeling power laws ($L(D) = E + B D^{-\beta}$), a 100M model on code **never** stays at $\approx 5.0\text{ CE}$ after 2.0 Billion real tokens and then suddenly collapses to $1.34\text{ CE}$ in the next 2.0 Billion tokens. The loss curve is convex and monotonic on log-log scales: the steepest drop occurs in the first 500M tokens.

The discrepancy between the reported **1:20 (4.89 CE)** and **1:40 (1.34 CE)** runs stems from **three compounding bugs in data loading, precision, and token accounting**:

---

#### Bug ①: The $8\times$ Data Duplication Deficit on TPU (Commit b19e133)
In the preliminary 100M run on Cloud TPU v3-8, 8 worker processes were spawned via `xmp.spawn`. Prior to commit b19e133, `idx_ptr` was initialized identically across all spawned workers without rank offsetting:
[fenced code/output: original retained]
* **What happened**: Every single TPU core was computing forward passes and backward gradients on the **exact same 48 sequences**!
* **The telemetry illusion**: The logger multiplied tokens by `world_size = 8`:
  $$\text{Tokens}_{\text{reported}} = 10,173 \text{ steps} \times 48 \times 8 \times 512 = \mathbf{2,000,093,184 \text{ tokens (2.0B / 1:20)}}$$
* **The ground truth**: Because all 8 cores processed identical data, the model only received:
  $$\text{Tokens}_{\text{unique}} = 10,173 \text{ steps} \times 48 \times 512 = \mathbf{250,011,648 \text{ tokens (0.25B)}}$$
* **The impact**: The 1:20 run had **not** seen 2.0B tokens. It had only seen **250M unique tokens**—a token-to-parameter ratio of **$1:2.5$**, not $1:20$. For a 100M model on code, 250M tokens is barely past basic syntax acquisition, which explains why validation CE was **4.89 nats (~5 CE)**.

---

#### Bug ②: The 1:40 AR Baseline was Truly 4.0B Tokens
The 100M Pure AR baseline was trained on Modal H100 with `batch_size = 384` for 20,346 steps on a single GPU.
* There was no multi-process data replication bug. Every step ingested 384 unique sequences ($196,608$ tokens/step).
* $\text{Tokens}_{\text{trained}} = 20,346 \times 384 \times 512 = \mathbf{4,000,094,208\text{ real unique tokens}}$ ($1:40$).
* At 4.0B unique tokens, the model completed its training trajectory and converged to **1.3407 CE** (PPL 3.82, Top-1 70.87%).
* **Conclusion**: The model did not drop from 5 CE to 1.3 CE between 2B and 4B tokens. It dropped from 5 CE to 1.3 CE between **250M tokens and 4.0B tokens** ($16\times$ more data).

---

#### Bug ③: AdamW Underflow in Native `bfloat16` (Commit a774173)
On the preliminary TPU run, model parameters in HBM were cast directly to `torch.bfloat16`.
* In `bfloat16`, the mantissa is only 7 bits, giving a machine epsilon of $\epsilon_{\text{mach}} = 2^{-7} \approx 0.0078125$.
* With learning rate $\eta = 3.46 \times 10^{-5}$, AdamW parameter update increments ($\Delta \theta \approx 10^{-5} \text{ to } 10^{-6}$) fell well below machine precision relative to weights of magnitude $\ge 0.1$.
* The weight updates were mathematically truncated to zero, stalling convergence at $\approx 4.89\text{ CE}$. (This was corrected in commit a774173 by keeping parameters in `float32` and utilizing hardware autocast).
