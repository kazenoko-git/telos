### 2. The 15% vs. 80% Mask Distribution Shock

- **During Training**: The infiller was trained on clean sequences with **15% random masks**. It learned to predict a missing token when 85% of the surrounding code was perfect ground truth.
- **At Inference**: The AR draft has an error rate of $\sim 85\%$.

There is no threshold where the infiller gets both a clean context and a familiar mask density.

### 3. Non-Autoregressive Multi-Token Collapse

When you run the 2nd pass:
$$\text{new\_tokens} = \text{model}(\text{corrupted\_sequence}, \text{mask\_override}=\text{False}).\text{argmax}(-1)$$
All `[MASK]` tokens are predicted **in parallel (non-autoregressively) in a single forward pass**.
- If token 10 and token 11 are both masked:
  - Token 10 predicts a word *without knowing what token 11 will choose*.
  - Token 11 predicts a word *without knowing what token 10 will choose*.
- Non-autoregressive generation famously suffers from **conditional independence failure**: you get repeated tokens, grammatically incompatible pairings, or incoherent identifier combinations.
- To fix multi-token errors properly, diffusion models require **10 to 50 iterative denoising steps** (which would make inference 20× to 50× slower). Doing it in 1 pass is mathematically incapable of coordinating adjacent corrections.
