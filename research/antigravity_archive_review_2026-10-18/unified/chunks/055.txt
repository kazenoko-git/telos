### 1. Conceptual & Mechanical Comparison

| Dimension | Pure AR Baseline | COROSred (Causal Pass + Infilling Pass) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Attention Geometry** | Strictly causal triangular mask ($M_{i,j} = -\infty$ for $j > i$). | **Dynamic**: Causal triangular mask in Pass 1; Full bidirectional mask ($M_{i,j} = 0$) in Pass 2. |
| **Contextual Visibility** | Left-to-right only ($x_{<t}$). Blind to subsequent code ($x_{>t}$). | **Omnidirectional**: Pass 1 sees past context; Pass 2 conditions simultaneously on **past AND future context**. |

| **Self-Correction** | **Zero**: An erroneous token cascades errors into all downstream tokens. | **Active Region Refinement**: Low-confidence spans are masked out and re-diffused using surrounding context. |
| **Compute Complexity** | $T$ sequential forward steps ($1.0\times$). | $T$ sequential steps (Pass 1) + **1 parallel bidirectional step** (Pass 2) ($\approx 1.1\times$ to $1.2\times$ FLOPs). |

### 2. How the 2-Pass COROSred Inference Loop Works

[fenced code/output: original retained]

1. **Pass 1 (Causal Generation + Error Detection)**:
   - The model generates initial draft tokens autoregressively under `mask_override=True`.
   - In the exact same forward pass, the **Learned Reliability Head (LRH)** outputs a scalar confidence score $r_i$ at every position.
   - Unlike AR models that rely on uncalibrated softmax entropy, the LRH is explicitly trained to separate correct tokens from errors. On our 15M validation slice, the LRH achieves **$85.04\%$ classification accuracy** with a $+0.50$ score separation margin between correct and wrong tokens.

2. **Pass 2 (Selective Region Refinement)**:
   - Instead of regenerating the sequence, the router identifies the subset of suspicious tokens and dilates them into coherent syntactic regions (avoiding single-token isolated edits).
   - The selected spans are replaced with `[MASK]`.
   - The model executes a single **parallel bidirectional infilling pass** (`mask_override=False`), allowing attention to attend to both the left context and the right context.

Both models in your 15M run were trained with an Effective Batch Size of **$\text{EBS} = 384$ sequences** ($48$ sequences per TPU core across 8 cores) and $T = 512$:

- **15M Pure AR Baseline**:
  $$\text{Tokens per step} = 384 \times 512 = \mathbf{196,608} \text{ tokens (100\% Causal)}$$

  $$\text{Infill tokens} = 96 \times 512 = 49,152 \text{ tokens (25\%) } [B_m]$$

### 3. Why Did COROSred Take 39.7 Mins vs. AR's 18.0 Mins?

If there are no duplicated forward passes and the token count is identical ($196,608$ tokens/step), why did training take $\approx 2.2\times$ longer?

1. **Two Kernel Invocations vs. One per Step (TPU MFU)**:
   - **AR**: Calls `model(batch)` once with a single static shape `(48, 512)` and a single causal FlashAttention/SDPA kernel. TPU TensorCore utilization is near peak (~680k tok/s).
   - **COROSred**: Calls `model()` twice per microbatch step with different tensor shapes:
     1. `model(B_c = 36, 512)` with `is_causal=True`
     2. `model(B_m = 12, 512)` with `is_causal=False`
     Because TPU v5e matrix units (MXUs) operate most efficiently on uniform power-of-two tensor dimensions, splitting the batch into $36$ and $12$ sub-batches incurs launch latency and slightly lower hardware Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU) (~315k tok/s).

2. **Learned Reliability Head Compute**:
   - COROSred computes an additional scalar projection head (`nn.Linear(256, 256) -> SiLU -> nn.Linear(256, 1)`) and its corresponding Binary Cross-Entropy backward pass on the causal sub-batch.

3. **Disk I/O and TPU Synchronization**:
   - COROSred saved checkpoints every 500 steps ($500, 1000, 1500, 2000, 2500, 3000, 3500$, final). On TPU VM hosts with `xm.rendezvous` barriers, state-dict serialization and disk writes added cumulative pause overhead.

- **In Inference**: AR uses **1 pass** ($T$ sequential steps), while COROSred uses **2 passes** ($T$ sequential causal steps + **1 parallel bidirectional step**), adding only $\approx 1.5\%$ wall-clock latency for the second pass.
