1. In extreme over-training regimes, gradient descent aggressively prioritizes easy, position-predictable tokens (literals at rank #32, operators at rank #67) to greedily minimize loss.
2. Because 25M parameters is a fixed representational budget, the network exhausts its capacity on structural syntax and starves the complex semantic heads needed for open-vocabulary naming (`self.config` vs `self.logger` at rank #3,954).
3. The loss drops on paper, but the model becomes a syntactic pattern-matcher rather than a semantic code understander.

This brings us to the fundamental limitation of standard Masked Discrete Diffusion: **the lack of self-correction.**

In an MDLM, once a token is unmasked during sampling, that decision is locked in permanently. If the model commits early to a structural template, it has no way to revise downstream semantic errors as the broader context comes into view.

By replacing absorbing mask states with uniform categorical noise, tokens can continuously transition across vocabularies during sampling. This introduces true **iterative self-correction** for code generation — giving the model the ability to fix mistakes instead of being trapped by them.
