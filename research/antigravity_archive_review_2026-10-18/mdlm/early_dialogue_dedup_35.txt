### 1. Systolic Array Tiling & Padding
The TPU v6e Trillium chip houses dual **256×256 systolic array Matrix Multiply Units (MXUs)**.
* In a massive model (e.g., Llama 70B), GEMM operations have dimensions of 4,096 to 8,192, easily saturating the 256×256 hardware grids and reaching 40–55% MFU.
* In our **25M architecture**, the hidden dimension is `384` and `head_dim=64`. When these small matrices are mapped onto the 256×256 systolic arrays, the hardware must zero-pad the dimensions to fit the matrix registers. This architectural mismatch naturally lowers the arithmetic intensity.

### 2. Memory Bandwidth vs. Compute Bound
In small transformers (<100M parameters), operations like **RoPE (Rotary Position Embeddings), RMSNorm, and residual additions** take up a proportionally larger fraction of runtime relative to the linear GEMMs. Small models are frequently memory-bandwidth bound rather than compute-bound.

### 3. Real-World MFU
Real-world research benchmarks for small models (~25M–50M) on TPU v5e/v6e typically achieve **5% to 12% MFU** (~25–50 TFLOPS sustained dense bfloat16 compute).
