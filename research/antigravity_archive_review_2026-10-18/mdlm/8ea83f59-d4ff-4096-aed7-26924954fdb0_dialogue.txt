### 🔍 Analysis of 2x T4 Support Requirements

1. **Multi-GPU Parallelization (`DataParallel`)**:
   - On Kaggle with **GPU T4 x2**, PyTorch detects `torch.cuda.device_count() == 2`.
   - Wrapping the model with `torch.nn.DataParallel(model)` splits the batch dimension across both T4 GPUs concurrently.
   - When saving checkpoints, accessing `model.module.state_dict()` ensures weights are saved cleanly without `module.` prefixes, preserving compatibility across all inference and evaluation scripts.

2. **Dynamic Multi-GPU Batch & Step Resolution**:
   - With 2 GPUs, the global batch size becomes `batch_size * 2` (e.g. $32 \times 2 = 64$).
   - The token scheduler automatically adjusts `max_steps` so that the total target tokens (e.g. 26.2M for 1:1 ratio) are trained in half the steps with 2x throughput.

3. **Turing T4 Precision Handling (`FP16` + `GradScaler`)**:
   - The NVIDIA Tesla T4 (Turing architecture, `sm_75`) does **not** have native hardware acceleration for `bfloat16` (`torch.cuda.is_bf16_supported() == False`).
   - For optimal Tensor Core utilization, the code should auto-detect whether `bf16` is supported; if not, it seamlessly uses `torch.float16` paired with `torch.amp.GradScaler('cuda')` to prevent underflow/overflow.

### The Math Checks Out

The forward process is a mixture of two CTMCs within each block:

$$Q_\varepsilon = (1 - \varepsilon) \cdot Q_{\text{absorb}} + \varepsilon \cdot Q_{\text{uniform}}$$

This is still a valid CTMC — linear combinations of rate matrices are rate matrices. The ELBO decomposes correctly because the forward marginal $q_\varepsilon(x_t | x_0)$ is well-defined at every $t$, and the loss reduces to predicting $x_0^i$ at every corrupted position regardless of corruption type:

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_t \sim q_\varepsilon}\left[\sum_i w(t) \cdot \log p_\theta(x_0^i \mid x_t, t, c_i) \cdot \mathbf{1}[x_t^i \neq x_0^i]\right]$$

where $c_i \in \{\text{mask}, \text{sub}\}$ is the per-token corruption type conditioning. The block decomposition adds the causal factorization across blocks:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{b=1}^{B} \mathcal{L}_{\text{block}}(b \mid \text{context}_{<b})$$

This is clean. No hand-waving needed.

2. **Long-form generation.** Full MDLM is $O(L^2)$ in sequence length for each denoising step. Block diffusion is $O(B^2 \times L/B) = O(BL)$ — linear in total sequence length. This matters for anything beyond 512 tokens.
