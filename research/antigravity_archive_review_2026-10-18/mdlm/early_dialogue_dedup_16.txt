The aggregate CE is lower because the easy categories improved so much they pulled the average down, but the hard categories actually **regressed**.

### 🧠 My Interpretation: Two Distinct Failure Modes

**1. Native RoPE Training Creates Positional Specialization**

The 25M 1:40 was trained **from scratch** with RoPE for all 7,630 steps. The attention heads have learned to exploit positional patterns extremely well — they know that after `=` comes a literal, after `import` comes a module name, after an operator comes an operand. These are **position-predictable** patterns.

But function names, attribute names, and class names are **semantically determined**, not positionally determined. You can't predict `self.logger` vs `self.config` from position alone — you need deep contextual understanding of what the class *does*. The model appears to have allocated capacity toward positional exploitation at the expense of semantic representation.

**2. The RoPE Fine-Tuned 50M Retained Semantic Knowledge**

The 50M 1:45 was originally trained *without* RoPE for 17,164 steps, then adapted with just 150 steps of RoPE fine-tuning. It retained its semantic representations from the original training and gained only the positional alignment on top. This gave it a **more balanced** capability profile — worse at easy stuff, much better at hard stuff.

### 💡 What This Means for Telos Going Forward

1. **The aggregate metrics (CE, Top-5) are misleading.** A model that perfectly predicts `=`, `(`, `)`, `True`, `False` but can't predict variable names is useless for code completion. You should probably **weight the category scores** or focus on the harder categories as the true quality signal.

2. **This actually validates the move to UDLM.** The reason MDLM struggles on naming/semantic tokens is exactly the "locked-in mistake" problem — the model must commit to a prediction in one shot with no ability to revise. Ψ-samplers and the self-correction mechanism in uniform noise diffusion would directly address the Function Names / Attribute Names collapse.

3. **The 50M tier needs more native RoPE training, not just fine-tuning.** The 150-step RoPE adaptation was clearly insufficient to unlock the full potential. If you trained a 50M model from scratch with RoPE at the 1:40 or 1:45 ratio, you'd likely see the same positional gains as 25M 1:40 but with enough capacity to also maintain the semantic predictions.

4. **The over-training scaling law is real but has diminishing returns.** Going from 1:35 → 1:40 at 25M improved CE by only 0.15 nats (7.39 → 7.24). The curve is flattening. You're approaching the point where more tokens won't help without either more parameters or a better training objective.

### 🎯 Bottom Line

The numbers look great on paper, but the model is becoming a **syntactic pattern matcher** rather than a **semantic code understander**. That's the natural ceiling of masked diffusion with this architecture scale. It's exactly what Deschenaux's work predicts — and exactly why moving to uniform noise or continuous diffusion is the right next step.
