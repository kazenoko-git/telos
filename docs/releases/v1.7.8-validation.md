# Télos v1.7.8 validation

## Metal fused projection and cross entropy

The opt-in MLX path keeps projection tiles in threadgroup memory and recomputes them in backward. On M5 with hidden widths divisible by 32, Metal tensor operations use 64 × 128 token and vocabulary tiles. During backward the rounded logits become their derivatives in place. The kernel reduces both gradient products over a whole tile before accumulating into FP32 output buffers. Other shapes and devices use the 32 × 64 SIMD-group path. The atomic accumulation order can vary, so checkpoint replays with this mode use tolerance-based comparisons. [Apple's Metal tensor documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/running-inline-ml-operations-in-a-shader-with-metal-4)

Measured on a 24 GB Apple M5 Pro after the other GPU job exited, with MLX 0.32.2, width 512, TF32 disabled, and compiled loss and both first-order gradients. For each shape, the six runs used the order full, original fused, optimized fused, optimized fused, original fused, full. Each run started a fresh process, excluded four warmups, and measured 12 synchronized calls. Latencies are pooled medians and allocations are maximum active MLX allocator peaks, including resident arrays.

| Precision | Tokens | Vocabulary | Full (ms) | Original fused (ms) | Optimized fused (ms) | Full (MiB) | Original fused (MiB) | Optimized fused (MiB) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| BF16 | 256 | 4,096 | 0.68 | 1.85 | 0.86 | 13.50 | 17.26 | 17.07 |
| BF16 | 1,024 | 8,192 | 1.77 | 12.80 | 3.20 | 54.02 | 38.03 | 36.53 |
| BF16 | 2,048 | 32,768 | 12.12 | 104.22 | 30.48 | 356.04 | 152.05 | 140.05 |
| BF16 | 2,048 | 65,024 | 24.92 | 202.57 | 54.74 | 703.04 | 293.80 | 269.99 |
| FP32 | 1,024 | 8,192 | 6.74 | 12.80 | 7.08 | 104.02 | 38.03 | 36.53 |

On the largest BF16 head, optimized fusion was 3.70× as fast as original fusion at a lower peak. It used 61.6% less peak allocation than the full head and took 2.20× as long. The smallest BF16 head used more memory than the full head. Across the sweep, maximum per-token cross-entropy error versus the full projection was below 2e-6 and relative L2 error for both gradients was below 7e-5. Tests cover compiled FP32 and BF16 gradients, partial tiles, a one-class vocabulary, a confident target with cross-entropy near 2.4e-11, and a BF16 AR training smoke run with gradient accumulation and checkpoint export.

A prototype that emitted vocabulary bands of gradients into MLX matrix multiplies took about 39 ms at the largest BF16 shape, but the allocator peak rose to 554 MiB despite graph dependencies between bands. Larger 128 × 128 and 64 × 256 tiles and a staggered tile dispatch failed to improve the 64 × 128 latency in the measured pilots. Explicitly reusing a Metal gradient-band buffer across native matrix multiplies could reduce atomic traffic while capping allocation.

The feature is opt-in. These measurements cover the head rather than full-size training throughput, peak allocation, or convergence. Older Apple devices and other MLX versions remain unbenchmarked for this change.
